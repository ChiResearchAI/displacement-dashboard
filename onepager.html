Displacement near high-frequency transit corridors

What the analysis measures, what it found, and what it cannot tell you on its own.

Demonstration  Every figure on this sheet is synthetic, generated from a fixed seed to exercise the template. It is not Metropolitan Council data, not Data Axle data, and not a finding about any Twin Cities community. This sheet shows the format a Task 5 one-pager takes, not a result.

What was measured

Household movement in and out of station areas along the region's high-frequency transit corridors, each year from 2008 to 2025, compared against control corridors without frequent transit. Movement is broken down by income relative to area median and by the phase the transit project had reached — pre-development, planning, construction, or operation.

Comparing a corridor against a control corridor is what separates displacement pressure from the ordinary level of household movement that happens everywhere.

Where values are missing

Some station areas carry no published figure. That is deliberate. A station area is suppressed when the number of households behind the figure falls below the publication threshold, because a small cell on a public map can identify a household.

A second station area in the same corridor may also be suppressed even when its own count is large enough. Without that, a single suppressed value could be recovered by subtracting the published ones from the corridor total.

A suppressed value is never shown as zero, and never left as a blank that the eye reads as “no displacement here.”

What this does not tell you

It does not tell you that any household was forced to move. Household movement is an indicator of displacement, not displacement itself. A consumer-data record cannot distinguish an eviction from a promotion.

It does not establish that transit caused the movement. Comparing a corridor against a control corridor narrows the field of explanations. It does not close it.

The Council's own Anti-Displacement Framework holds that displacement is ultimately defined by the residents of an impacted community. This analysis is one input to that conversation, not a substitute for it.
